Janice Chang · PM Interview · Internal Tools

Publish with confidence, at scale, every single time.

Publishing failures, data inaccuracies, and a clunky UI have eroded trust in the CMS. As volume grows, so does the cost of errors — and today's CMS offers no tools to prevent them. AI is an afterthought, not a collaborator.

Who we're solving for
Internal CMS Editor
Accountable for everything that goes live — quality, timing, and brand voice. Not a developer, but comfortable with technology and increasingly expected to do more with less. Sees AI as a genuine opportunity, but frustrated that today's tools don't integrate it meaningfully into the workflow.
Product vision
🤖
AI-native workflows
AI embedded in drafting, review, tagging, and QA — not bolted on after the fact.
✅
Guaranteed delivery
Scheduling visibility, alerts, and clarity so CMS teams always know what's live, what's pending, and what needs action.
⚡
Modern, fast UI
An interface that surfaces the right data at the right moment — reducing the learning curve and the manual work.
No single view of what's live, pending, or flagged
Editors have no central place to see the state of their content — what's current, what's been flagged, what's waiting for review. Problems surface through reader complaints, not the system.
Solution
A dashboard that gives editors one view of content health — scored, prioritized, and always current — so they spend time fixing problems, not hunting for them.

Errors reach readers before the system catches them
When content is wrong or outdated, readers notice first — not the CMS. In r/CreditCards threads, the feedback is blunt: "take it with a grain of salt," "heavy affiliate bias," "TPG is for basics." Trust erodes before the team even knows there's a problem.
Solution
An AI assistant that cites its sources and reads directly from CMS-verified data — so readers see what the system knows, not just what the article says.
💬 AI Chat Panel Open demo →
Outdated tooling slows onboarding and keeps manual work in place
New team members face a clunky interface with no standard workflows, extending ramp time. Meanwhile, manual tagging and enrichment persist because the tools don't support anything better — and the AI is only as reliable as the data behind it.
Solution
A CLI engineers use to audit, enrich, and update article metadata at scale — reducing manual overhead and keeping the content graph accurate enough for AI to read reliably.

How we'd evaluate this

Publishing speed
Time-to-publish per article · editorial throughput per writer per week · time-to-first-draft with AI scaffolding
Accuracy before publish
% stale articles caught before reader complaints · affiliate link rot rate · mean time-to-update after a valuation change
Manual work reduced
Reduction in manual audit time · tagging accuracy with AI assistance · content enrichment coverage across the catalog
